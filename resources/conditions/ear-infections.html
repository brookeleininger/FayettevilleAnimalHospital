Ear Infections in Dogs and Cats

Understand signs of ear inflammation and why recurring infections need a closer look.

Important: This guide supports informed observation but does not provide a diagnosis.

What owners may notice

Head shaking, scratching, odor, redness, debris, or pain can occur with infection, allergies, mites, foreign material, or other disease.

Write down when signs began, how often they occur, recent changes, and any photos or videos that may help. Avoid giving leftover or human medication unless a veterinarian directs you.

Why an examination matters

Avoid placing leftover medication or harsh cleaners in a painful ear. An exam and ear sample help guide safe, targeted treatment.

Different conditions can look alike from home. An examination helps narrow the possibilities and determine whether testing, treatment, monitoring, or referral is appropriate.

When to seek care

Prompt care is appropriate when signs cause pain, recur, worsen, interfere with eating or movement, or come with marked lethargy. Trouble breathing, collapse, uncontrolled bleeding, major trauma, prolonged seizures, or inability to urinate require emergency care.
